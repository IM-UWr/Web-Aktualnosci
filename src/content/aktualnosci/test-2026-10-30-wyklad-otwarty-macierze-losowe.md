## Abstract

<div lang="en">

In 1972 a conversation over tea revealed that the pair correlation of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function, conjectured by Montgomery, coincides with the pair correlation of eigenvalues of large random Hermitian matrices studied by physicists. Since then random matrix theory has become one of the most reliable sources of conjectures in analytic number theory: it predicts the moments of $\zeta(1/2 + it)$, the distribution of gaps between zeros, and statistics of whole families of $L$-functions. In the lecture I will explain, with many pictures and few formulas, why eigenvalues of random unitary matrices "repel" each other, what this has to do with primes, and which of these predictions have actually been proved.
